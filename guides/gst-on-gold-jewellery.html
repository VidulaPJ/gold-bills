GST on gold jewellery: is it 3% or 5% on making charges?

Updated 24 November 2026

In short: when you buy jewellery from a jeweller, GST is 3% of the total value of the jewellery, including making charges, whether or not making charges are shown separately. That is the answer in the GST Council's own FAQ on gems and jewellery. 5% is the rate for job work, for example a goldsmith making jewellery from gold you give them, or repairs.

How 3% is shown on the bill

Within your own state, the 3% is split into 1.5% CGST + 1.5% SGST. If the shop is in another state (for example an online order), it's shown as 3% IGST.

Gold value10.000 g × ₹12,850/g₹1,28,500
Making charges10.000 g × ₹950/g₹9,500
Taxable value₹1,38,000
CGST 1.5%₹2,070
SGST 1.5%₹2,070
Bill total₹1,42,140

The “3% + 5%” mistake

Many websites say GST is 3% on gold plus 5% on making charges. For a normal jewellery purchase that's not what the GST Council's FAQ says: it asks exactly this question (3% on the total, or 3% on gold and 5% on making?) and answers 3% of the total transaction value.

On the example above, 3% on gold + 5% on making would come to ₹4,330 instead of ₹4,140: ₹190 more. On a bigger wedding purchase the difference grows. If your bill charges 5% on making, ask the jeweller which rule they're using.

When other rates apply

Repairs and other services can be taxed differently. Rates can change: check the GST Council's FAQ on gems and jewellery for the official wording.
