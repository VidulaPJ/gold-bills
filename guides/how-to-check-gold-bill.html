How to check a gold jewellery bill

Updated 24 November 2026

In short: for each item, net weight × rate + making charges should equal the item's amount; the items should add up to the taxable value; GST is 3% of that; and the grand total should be the sum. Then check the purity and the HUID on the bill match what's stamped on the jewellery.

A correct bill, worked through

A 22K gold chain, 10.000 g, at the bill's 22K rate of ₹12,850 per gram, with making charges of ₹950 per gram:

Gold value10.000 g × ₹12,850/g₹1,28,500
Making charges10.000 g × ₹950/g₹9,500
Taxable value₹1,38,000
CGST 1.5%₹2,070
SGST 1.5%₹2,070
Bill total₹1,42,140

Your bill may round the grand total to the nearest rupee. A difference of a few paise is normal; a difference of hundreds or thousands of rupees is not.

The 7 checks

1. Net weight, not gross weight

Gold is charged on net weight: the weight of gold only. If the piece has stones, beads or a thread, the gross weight includes them. Make sure the gold rate is multiplied by the net weight, and that stones are priced separately, by their own weight.

2. Purity matches the hallmark

The purity on the bill (for example 22K916, 18K750 or 14K585) should match the hallmark stamped on the item. 916 means 91.6% gold, 750 means 75%. You should not pay the 22K rate for 18K jewellery. How to read a hallmark →

3. The rate charged matches the rate printed on the bill

Most bills show the day's rate at the top (“Today's rate: 24K ₹… 22K ₹…”). The rate used on each item line should be the same as the printed rate for that purity. If a line uses a higher rate than the shop's own printed rate, ask why.

4. Making charges are what you agreed

Making charges can be written per gram, as a percentage of the gold value, or as a flat amount. Work them out as a percentage so you can compare shops: in the example, ₹9,500 ÷ ₹1,28,500 = 7.4%. Making charges and wastage explained →

5. Each line adds up

For every item: net weight × rate = gold value; gold value + making (+ stones, if any) = the line's amount. A typing error in one line is the easiest mistake to miss.

6. GST is 3% of the total value

For jewellery bought from a jeweller, GST is 3% of the whole value, including the making charges (shown as 1.5% CGST + 1.5% SGST, or 3% IGST from another state). Why it's 3%, not 3% + 5% →

7. The grand total is the sum

Add the items, add GST, subtract any discount or old-gold exchange value: that is what you should pay. Check the amount you actually paid (card, UPI, cash) matches the grand total, and the amount written in words matches the number.

Before you leave the shop

  • Ask for a proper tax invoice with the shop's GSTIN, the date, and each item listed separately.
  • Check the HUID (6 characters) for each item is on the bill and matches the one stamped on the jewellery. You can verify it in the official BIS Care app.
  • Keep the bill. You'll need it to exchange, resell or insure the jewellery, and to prove purity if there's a dispute.
