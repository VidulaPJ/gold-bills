Gold making charges explained

Updated 24 November 2026

In short: making charges pay for turning gold into jewellery. Shops write them in different ways, so convert them to one number: making ÷ gold value, as a percentage. That's the only fair way to compare two shops or two designs.

Three ways shops write making charges

Per gram

For example ₹950 per gram. On 10.000 g: 10.000 g × ₹950 = ₹9,500.

As a percentage of the gold value

For example 8% of the gold value. On a gold value of ₹1,28,500: 8% = ₹10,280.

As wastage or “VA” (value addition)

Common in South India. Wastage is extra gold you pay for, written as a percentage of the weight or as extra grams. For example 10 g with 8% wastage means you pay the gold rate for 10.8 g. It's the same idea as a percentage making charge, just added to the weight.

Turn any of them into one percentage

Making % = making charges ÷ gold value × 100

Example: ₹9,500 ÷ ₹1,28,500 × 100 = 7.4%. For wastage, the percentage is the wastage itself (8% wastage ≈ 8% making).

What affects making charges

  • Design: plain chains and bangles usually cost less to make than intricate, handmade or antique-finish pieces.
  • Machine-made or handmade.
  • The shop: big brands and local jewellers price differently, and many shops reduce making charges during festival offers.

Making charges are often negotiable. Ask for them in writing before the bill is made, and check the bill uses the same figure.

GST on making charges

When you buy jewellery from a jeweller, GST is 3% of the gold value and the making charges together. More about GST on gold →
